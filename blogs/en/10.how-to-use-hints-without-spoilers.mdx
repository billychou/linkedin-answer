There is a quiet tension in every daily-puzzle habit: the streak demands a solve, but looking up the answer kills the satisfaction. Most players swing between the extremes — white-knuckling alone or pasting the answer immediately — and both waste what answer sites are actually good for. This is the workflow we designed LinkedIn Answer Today around: hints first, answers last.

## The three levels of help

Think of help as a ladder, and climb it only as far as you need:

1. **A nudge.** A hint that points your attention somewhere — "look at the smallest colored region", "count the cells between waypoints 2 and 3". You still do the solving.
2. **A method.** A step-by-step walkthrough of *how* the solution can be found, read after you have solved (or after you have genuinely given up). This is the level that actually makes you better at the game.
3. **The answer itself.** The completed grid, the ladder, the queen positions. Useful for verification, streak rescue, or when you are simply out of time.

Most players default to level 3 because it is one click away. But level 1 preserves the fun and level 2 builds the skill — and a site that offers all three lets you choose per day, per mood, per puzzle.

## A streak-friendly routine

1. **Play the puzzle straight** for a few minutes. Most days that is all you need.
2. **Stuck?** Ask for one hint — a nudge, not the answer. Apply it and keep playing.
3. **Still stuck after two hints?** Take the answer. A solved puzzle with a saved streak beats an abandoned one.
4. **After solving, read the explanation anyway.** Comparing your path against another valid one is the cheapest skill training there is.
5. **On weekends, drill the archives.** Past puzzles are perfect for practicing a specific game's techniques without pressure.

## Why "hints first" actually wins

The fun of these games is the moment the pattern clicks. A hint preserves that moment — it narrows your search space but leaves the deduction to you. An answer erases it. Over a month, the hints-first player gets faster; the answers-first player gets the same streak with none of the growth, and usually quits when the puzzles stop feeling rewarding.

Use the ladder. Nudge first, method second, answer last — and keep the click for the days you genuinely need it.
