Pinpoint is LinkedIn's flagship word game, and it is deceptively simple: five clues, one hidden connection. Yet most players do not solve it until clue four or five. The difference between a slow solve and an early guess is not vocabulary — it is method. This guide covers the rules and the strategies that actually move the needle.

## The rules

- You are shown clues one at a time, up to five.
- All five clues are connected by a single common category or theme.
- Your goal is to identify that connection as early as possible.
- Each clue is a word or a short phrase. Nothing is random — every clue fits the hidden theme.

A typical theme might be "things that can be cracked" (egg, code, safe, smile, dawn) — but you will never see the theme stated. You infer it from the evidence.

## Why the first clues feel impossible

Early clues are deliberately broad. A clue like "bank" could belong to dozens of themes: rivers, money, slopes, billiards. The game is designed so that each additional clue narrows the space of plausible themes. Your job with clue one is not to solve — it is to start collecting candidate themes.

## Five strategies that work

### 1. Generate multiple interpretations, not one

For each new clue, list at least three different meanings or associations. "Bat" is an animal, sports equipment, and a verb. Players who lock onto the first interpretation get anchored and miss the theme when later clues arrive.

### 2. Find the intersection, not the pattern

The theme is whatever set contains all clues so far. After two clues, write down candidate categories that include both. After three clues, most wrong candidates die on their own. If a candidate cannot fit a new clue, drop it without sentiment.

### 3. Watch for category signals

Themes usually come from a small number of families: compound words (sun___), phrases ("___ of war"), things with a shared physical property, homophones, or idioms. If two clues form a compound word with a third word, test that third word against the remaining clues immediately.

### 4. Use the clues' specificity

A very specific clue ("sonnet") is a stronger constraint than a broad one ("poem"). Attack the puzzle from its most specific clue and check the others against it.

### 5. Read your guess out loud

Themes are often phrased as "things that ___" or "___ can be cracked". If your candidate phrase does not naturally complete every clue, it is probably not the theme even if it fits two or three clues.

## What to do when you are stuck

- Re-read every clue as if you had never seen it. Anchoring is the number one cause of stalls.
- Ask: could any clue be a verb, a name, or part of a compound word?
- If you still have no thread after clue four, look at today's answer explanation — our daily [Pinpoint answer page](/games/pinpoint) includes a breakdown of how the clues connect, which trains exactly the pattern-recognition this game rewards.

## Building a streak

Pinpoint publishes one puzzle every day, and your streak counts solves, not perfect early guesses. A five-clue solve keeps the streak alive. Play the same time each day, take the guess you can defend, and let the early-guess skill come with practice.
